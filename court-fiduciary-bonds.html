Court, Probate & Fiduciary Bonds in Colorado

Appointed by a court to manage someone else’s estate or affairs? A fiduciary bond is often the next step, and we can make it painless.

Bonds written through CNA Surety

When a court gives you authority over someone else’s money or property, it often asks for a guarantee that you will handle it honestly and by the rules. That guarantee is a fiduciary bond, and it is one of the most common reasons an individual, not a business, needs a surety bond. It is typical for executors and personal representatives of an estate, guardians of a minor or incapacitated person, and conservators who manage another person’s finances.

Other court bonds are required of people who ask a court for a remedy, such as an appeal, an injunction or the seizure of property, so the other side is protected if the request turns out to be unjustified. Reardon Agency places court and fiduciary bonds with CNA Surety, which lists both court bonds and fiduciary bonds among its commercial bond lines.

Fiduciary Bonds

A fiduciary is someone legally trusted to manage another person’s or an estate’s assets. The bond protects the people who depend on the fiduciary.

📜

Executor / Personal Representative Bonds

Guarantees the person named to administer a probate estate will inventory assets, pay debts and distribute property properly. Required when a will demands it or the court orders it.

🧒

Guardian Bonds

Protects a minor or an incapacitated person, and often covers any money or property the guardian handles on their behalf.

💼

Conservator Bonds

Protects the finances of a person who cannot manage their own money. Courts commonly require a bond sized to the assets under management.

🏦

Trustee & Receiver Bonds

Where a court appoints a trustee, receiver or similar officer over assets, a bond may be a condition of the appointment.

Court Bonds

These bonds are posted by people involved in a lawsuit so the other side is covered if things go the other way.

📬

Appeal (Supersedeas) Bonds

Posted to pause enforcement of a judgment while it is appealed, guaranteeing payment if the appeal fails.

🛑

Injunction & Attachment Bonds

Posted when you ask the court to stop an action or hold property, covering the other side’s losses if the request was wrongful.

📦

Replevin & Other Litigation Bonds

Posted to recover specific property or meet another court requirement during a case.

Availability and amounts depend on the court order and on CNA Surety’s underwriting. Larger or unusual court bonds are reviewed individually. Send the order and Ryan will tell you what is possible.

How It Works for a Probate or Guardianship Bond

The court decides whether a bond is needed. A will may say that the executor must be bonded or may waive the bond. The court has the final say, and may require a bond based on the size and type of the assets, the fiduciary’s circumstances and the interested parties. In Colorado, ask the court or your attorney whether a bond is required in your case.

The bond amount is set by the court and is usually linked to the value of the assets the fiduciary will control. The premium is typically paid annually and can often be charged to the estate rather than to you personally.

Underwriting reviews the fiduciary. Because the surety is guaranteeing a person’s honesty with someone else’s money, it looks at the individual’s credit and background. Strong credit means a smoother, cheaper bond.

Have ready

  • The court order or the letters of appointment
  • The bond amount the court set
  • Your name, address and the case or estate name
  • The county and court where the case was filed
  • An estimate of the assets involved

Just lost the original paperwork?

If the issue is a missing original note, deed of trust or share certificate rather than a court appointment, you need a different bond. See lost note and water stock bonds. We are not attorneys and cannot give legal advice. Your probate or guardianship attorney can tell you whether a bond is required and in what amount.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to the questions we hear most.

What is a fiduciary bond?

A fiduciary bond is a surety bond that guarantees a person appointed by a court, such as an executor, guardian or conservator, will manage another person’s assets honestly and according to the law. If the fiduciary misuses the assets, the bond can be used to reimburse the estate or the person being protected.

Does an executor need to be bonded in Colorado?

Not always. Many wills waive the bond requirement, and a court decides whether one is needed in a given estate. If a bond is required, Ryan can place it. Ask the probate court or your attorney whether a bond is required in your case.

How much does a probate or fiduciary bond cost?

The premium is a small percentage of the bond amount, typically charged annually, and it depends on the bond amount and the fiduciary’s credit. Because the bond amount is tied to the size of the assets, a larger estate means a larger premium. The cost can often be paid from the estate.

What is a guardian or conservator bond?

A guardian or conservator bond is required by the court when someone is appointed to care for, or manage the money of, a minor or incapacitated person. It protects the ward against mismanagement or theft by the appointee.

What is an appeal bond or supersedeas bond?

An appeal bond, also called a supersedeas bond, is posted by a party that is appealing a court judgment and wants to delay paying it. It guarantees the judgment will be paid if the appeal is lost. These bonds are typically sized to the judgment, so they are reviewed individually.

Can I get a court bond with bad credit?

It depends on the bond and on the amount. Fiduciary bonds look closely at the individual’s credit. Tell Ryan early so the right underwriting approach can be used, and be ready to provide extra information if the bond is large.
