Flood Insurance — Shopped Across Multiple Carriers

Standard homeowners insurance never covers flood damage. Ryan writes flood coverage through NFIP (via Farmers) and private carriers like Neptune — residential, commercial, and HOA — and shops pricing across all of them to find the best fit.

Most homeowners find out the hard way: a standard home insurance policy does not cover flood damage, full stop. It's a separate policy — and most people assume they don't need it because they don't live near a river or in a mapped flood zone. In Colorado, that assumption causes real problems, since flash flooding and sudden, intense storms can affect homes well outside any official flood zone.

Ryan writes flood coverage through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) via Farmers, plus private-market carriers like Neptune and others — and shops pricing across them rather than defaulting to just one option. Coverage is available for individual homes, commercial properties, and HOAs.

Flood Coverage Options

Three ways to get covered, shopped and compared so you're not just stuck with whatever one carrier offers.

Commercial & HOA Flood

Why This Isn't Optional to Think About

  • 🏡
    Your Home Policy Doesn't Cover It

    Flood is explicitly excluded from standard homeowners and most commercial property policies. It's always a separate policy.

  • 📍
    You Don't Have to Be in a Mapped Flood Zone

    A large share of flood claims nationally come from outside high-risk flood zones — flash flooding doesn't check the map first.

  • 🏔️
    Colorado-Specific Risk

    Sudden, intense storms, mountain runoff, and burn-scar flooding after wildfires (where burned terrain can no longer absorb water) all raise flood risk here in ways many residents don't expect.

  • 💰
    Price Competition Matters

    Shopping NFIP against private carriers like Neptune can turn up meaningfully different pricing for similar coverage — it's worth comparing rather than assuming one option is cheapest.
