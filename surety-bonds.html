What Is a Surety Bond? Three Parties, One Promise

Every surety bond has the same three players. Once you see who is who, the rest makes sense.

Party 1

The Principal

That is you: the contractor, business or individual who needs the bond and promises to perform.

Party 2

The Obligee

The party that requires the bond and is protected by it: a city, county, state agency, project owner, court or customer.

Party 3

The Surety

The company that guarantees your promise. Here that is CNA Surety. If you default, the surety steps in, and then looks to you to be repaid.

Surety Bond vs. Insurance

They are not the same product, and the difference matters when something goes wrong.

Insurance policySurety bond
Who is protectedYou, the policyholderThe obligee (the party requiring the bond)
Number of partiesTwo: you and the insurerThree: principal, obligee and surety
If there is a claimThe insurer pays and does not expect repaymentThe surety may pay, then the principal must reimburse the surety
How it is underwrittenExpected losses across many policyholdersCloser to a line of credit: character, credit and finances
What the premium buysCoverage for future lossesThe surety’s guarantee of your promise

How Much Does a Surety Bond Cost?

You do not pay the full bond amount. You pay a premium, a small percentage of the bond, and the price depends on the kind of bond, the amount and your financial strength.

Contract bonds for contractors with strong credit and financials are typically priced at a low single-digit percentage of the bond amount, and CNA Surety’s Fast Track programs use account-based premiums. Fidelity bonds are priced from a published rate table. For example, a basic one-year employee dishonesty bond with $5,000 of coverage for a business with five or fewer employees starts around $100 a year (see the employee dishonesty rate table). Some bonds, like lost note bonds, are priced on the amount at risk.

The only way to know your price is to ask. Quotes are free, and there is no pressure.

What affects your price

  • The type of bond and the dollar amount
  • Personal and business credit
  • Financial statements (for larger contract bonds)
  • Years in business and work history
  • Claims or losses in the past few years
  • Whether the bond is eligible for an instant-issue program

Surety Bond Questions, Answered

Straight answers to the questions we hear most.

What is a surety bond?

A surety bond is a three-party agreement in which a surety company guarantees that a person or business (the principal) will meet an obligation to another party (the obligee). The obligation might be finishing a construction project, following a licensing law, protecting a customer’s property or properly managing someone else’s estate. If the principal does not follow through, the obligee can make a claim on the bond.

Is a surety bond the same as insurance?

No. Insurance protects the policyholder. A surety bond protects the party that requires the bond, and the principal is responsible for repaying the surety for any valid claim. Because the surety expects to be reimbursed, bonds are underwritten more like credit than insurance.

How much does a surety bond cost?

You pay a premium, not the full bond amount. For contract bonds the premium is usually a small percentage of the bond, and it is lower for contractors with strong credit and financial statements. Fidelity bonds follow a published rate table, and a basic $5,000 employee dishonesty bond for a very small business starts around $100 a year. Ask for a free quote for your exact bond.

How long does it take to get a surety bond?

It depends on the bond. Many small commercial bonds can be approved the same day, and CNA Surety offers instant issue on selected bonds with approved credit, such as highway permit and right-of-way bonds up to $250,000 and H-2A farm labor bonds up to $150,000. Contract bonds are quick for repeat clients and take longer the first time while financial information is reviewed.

Can I get bonded with a new business or less-than-perfect credit?

Often, yes. Credit is one factor, not the only one. CNA Surety’s credit-based Fast Track program considers contractors in their first year of business ownership for jobs up to $250,000. If your credit is a challenge, tell Ryan up front so the request goes to the right program and any additional information, like financial statements or collateral, can be lined up in advance.

What is the difference between a bid bond, a performance bond and a payment bond?

A bid bond guarantees that the low bidder will sign the contract and provide the required bonds. A performance bond guarantees the contractor will finish the work as the contract requires. A payment bond guarantees that subcontractors, laborers and suppliers will be paid. Public projects usually require performance and payment bonds together. Read the full contractor bond guide.

Do you write surety bonds outside Colorado Springs?

Yes. Reardon Agency is based in Colorado Springs and works with bond principals throughout Colorado, from Pueblo and the Pikes Peak region to the Denver metro area. If your bond is required in another state, call and Ryan will tell you honestly whether the agency can help.

What do I need to apply for a bond?

For most small bonds, your name or business name, contact information, the bond form or requirement and permission for a credit check are enough. Larger contract bonds also use business financial statements and personal financial statements for the owners. Ryan will tell you exactly what is needed once he knows which bond you are requesting.
