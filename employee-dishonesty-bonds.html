Employee Dishonesty Bonds for Colorado Businesses

Protect your business, your customers and your reputation from employee theft with a blanket employee dishonesty bond, including non-profit and janitorial service bonds up to $200,000.

Bonds written through CNA Surety

Most employees are honest, but you cannot know which ones will not be. An employee dishonesty bond (also called a fidelity bond) reimburses you for money, securities or property stolen by your employees, up to the coverage limit you choose. For service businesses it can also protect the customers whose homes and offices your team enters, which is why many clients ask for proof of bonding before they sign a contract.

Small organizations are the most exposed: the Association of Certified Fraud Examiners’ 2024 study estimates that a typical organization loses about 5% of revenue to fraud each year, and smaller organizations are less likely to have anti-fraud controls in place. A bond turns a potentially business-ending loss into a covered claim. How the Bond Works

CNA Surety’s Employee Dishonesty Bond is a blanket position bond, which keeps it simple.

Blanket coverage. One bond covers every employee, full-time and part-time, up to the limit you select. You do not have to name people or list positions, and new hires are automatically included. You count all employees when the premium is calculated.

Two classes of business. Class A is for professional and business offices such as accountants, architects, physicians, dentists, insurance agents and attorneys. Class B is for higher-exposure businesses such as cafes, gas stations, retail stores, courier services and businesses with salespeople. The Class B bond includes a conviction clause: it requires the criminal conviction of the dishonest employee, which also protects your honest employees from unfair accusations.

Officers and owners. Officers of a corporation are covered only if they are in the regular service of the business and paid salary or wages, and covering owners and officers is subject to underwriter approval.

Choose a term. Buy a one-year bond, or prepay three years at a reduced rate (2.85 times the annual premium) on the Class B bond.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to the questions we hear most.

What is an employee dishonesty bond?

An employee dishonesty bond is a fidelity bond that reimburses a business for losses caused by dishonest acts of its employees, such as theft or embezzlement, up to the bond limit. It is usually written as a blanket bond that covers all employees, full-time and part-time.

How much does an employee dishonesty bond cost?

CNA Surety’s published rates start around $100 a year for $5,000 of coverage with five or fewer employees, about $187 for $25,000 and about $359 for $100,000. The premium goes up with the number of employees and the limit you choose. See the rate table above, and ask Ryan for an exact quote.

What is a janitorial service bond?

A janitorial service bond is a form of dishonesty bond that protects a cleaning company’s customers from theft by the company’s employees. Many commercial and residential customers ask cleaning companies for proof of bonding before hiring them. CNA Surety offers janitorial service bonds with limits up to $200,000.

Is an employee dishonesty bond required for non-profits?

Not by law in most cases, but boards, grantmakers, banks and insurers often expect it, and it is considered a best practice for organizations that handle donations. CNA Surety’s non-profit dishonesty bonds are available with limits up to $200,000.

What is the difference between Class A and Class B dishonesty bonds?

Class A is for professional and business offices such as accountants, attorneys, architects, doctors, dentists and insurance agents. Class B is for businesses with more exposure, such as cafes, gas stations, retail stores, businesses with salespeople and courier services. Class B includes a conviction clause that requires the criminal conviction of the dishonest employee.

Does the bond cover the business owner?

Employees are covered. Officers of a corporation are covered only if they are in the regular service of the business and compensated by salary or wages, and covering owners and officers requires underwriter approval. Tell Ryan about your ownership structure so the bond is written correctly.

Is an employee dishonesty bond the same as being bonded and insured?

People use “bonded and insured” loosely. “Insured” usually means you carry general liability insurance, and “bonded” means you carry a surety or fidelity bond that protects customers from employee theft. Many service companies carry both, and Reardon Agency can provide both.

How fast can I get an employee dishonesty bond?

The application is short, and most requests are underwritten quickly. Class A limits of $100,000 or more need extra information on audits, bank reconciliation and check-signing controls, so allow a bit more time for those.
