The Contract Bonds We Write

Most projects use a combination. Public jobs commonly ask for a performance bond and a payment bond together once the contract is awarded.

📝

Bid Bond

Guarantees that if you are the low bidder you will sign the contract at your bid price and furnish the performance and payment bonds. Typically a percentage of your bid, or a flat amount.

✅

Performance Bond

Guarantees you will complete the work according to the contract. If you cannot, the surety can arrange for the job to be finished.

💵

Payment Bond

Guarantees that your subcontractors, laborers and material suppliers are paid. It also gives them a way to be paid on public projects, where liens are not available.

🔧

Maintenance (Warranty) Bond

Covers defects in workmanship or materials for a set period after the project is complete, often one to two years. Available as a stand-alone bond.

📦

Supply Bond

Guarantees that a supplier will deliver the materials or equipment promised under a supply contract.

🤝

Subcontractor Bond

Performance and payment bonds that a general contractor requires from subcontractors, so the whole project chain is protected.

What You Need to Get a Contract Bond

The smaller the job, the lighter the paperwork. Here is what each level usually involves.

  • Credit review of the owners
  • Details of the job and who requires the bond
📊

Jobs up to $1,000,000 (Fast Track Pro)

  • Two-page application with financial data
  • Last two fiscal years of company financial statements (CPA-prepared or in-house balance sheet and income statement)
  • Owners’ personal financial statements
  • Work history and bank line-of-credit information
🖋️

New to bonding

  • A general indemnity agreement signed by the owners (and spouses, if owners)
  • Copy of the contract or bid documents for larger jobs
  • The obligee’s bond form, if they require a specific one

Fast Track Pro is built for incorporated contractors (C-corp, S-corp and LLC). Sole proprietors and partnerships can ask about other options.

Colorado public works: when a bond is required

  • Local government projects over $50,000. Colorado law (C.R.S. 38-26-105 and 38-26-106) requires a payment bond, and a performance bond, from the contractor on public works contracts above $50,000 awarded by a county, municipality, school district or other political subdivision. The performance bond must be at least half of the contract amount.
  • State projects over $150,000. The same payment and performance bond requirements apply to contracts awarded by the State of Colorado above $150,000.
  • Private entities on public property. Contracts over $150,000 with a private entity for work on publicly owned property are covered too.
  • Federal projects. The federal Miller Act requires performance and payment bonds on federal construction contracts above $150,000.

Subcontractors and suppliers generally have six months after the work is completed to sue on a Colorado payment bond, which is one reason project owners take bond requirements seriously. Thresholds and rules change, so Ryan confirms the requirement on your actual project before quoting. This is general information, not legal advice.

What Do Contract Bonds Cost?

The premium is a percentage of the contract amount (or of the bond penalty), and it is usually a low single-digit percentage for contractors with solid credit and financial strength. It can be higher for newer contractors or those with weaker credit or financials, and lower as your track record builds.

CNA Surety’s Fast Track Pro uses competitive account-based premiums across all of your contract bonds, so the more you bond with the same program, the more consistent your pricing and process become. Many contractors also find that a clean financial picture and a strong insurance program improve the quote.

The premium is a fee for the guarantee, not a deductible. You do not get it back, but you do not “use up” the bond either. If a claim is ever made, the surety expects you to reimburse it.

Ways to get a better bond rate

  • Keep personal and business credit healthy
  • Provide current, accurate financial statements
  • Show a track record of completed jobs
  • Keep general liability and workers’ comp in good order
  • Bring us the job early, before the bid is due

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to the questions we hear most.

What is a contract surety bond?

A contract surety bond guarantees that a contractor will meet the terms of a construction contract. The most common types are the bid bond, the performance bond and the payment bond. They protect the project owner (the obligee) and, in the case of payment bonds, the subcontractors and suppliers on the job.

When do I need a bid bond?

You need a bid bond when the project owner requires one with your bid, which is common on public work and many larger private projects. It assures the owner that if you win, you will sign the contract and provide the performance and payment bonds. Bid bonds are usually a percentage of your bid amount, and with Fast Track they are often quick to issue.

Do I need a performance bond and a payment bond?

On most public projects, yes. In Colorado, local public works over $50,000 and state projects over $150,000 call for a payment bond and a performance bond, and the performance bond must be at least half of the contract. Private owners and general contractors may also require them, so check your contract documents or send them to Ryan.

How much does a bid, performance or payment bond cost?

The premium is a percentage of the contract amount, typically a low single-digit percentage for contractors with strong credit and financials. New contractors or those with credit challenges may pay more. Fast Track Pro offers account-based premiums across all contract bonds, and the exact rate is quoted once the application is reviewed.

Can a new contractor get bonded?

Yes, within limits. CNA Surety’s credit-based Fast Track program considers entry-level contractors in their first year of business ownership for jobs up to $250,000. As you finish jobs and build financial history, you can move toward the larger programs.

What is the difference between Fast Track and Fast Track Pro?

Fast Track is credit-based and works for jobs up to $750,000 with an online application and credit-based underwriting rather than a full financial-statement package. Fast Track Pro raises the limits to $1,000,000 for a single bond and $1,500,000 in aggregate and offers improved rates, but it requires a two-page application with company and personal financial information.

How long does it take to get a contract bond?

Repeat clients can often get bonds the same day or the next business day. First-time applicants take longer because the underwriter reviews credit and, for larger programs, financial statements and signed indemnity paperwork. Contact Ryan as soon as you decide to bid, since many bids have deadlines for submitting bid bonds.

Does a Colorado public works contractor need a surety bond for a school or city project?

Generally yes. Colorado’s Public Works Act requires a payment bond and a performance bond for contracts above $50,000 with counties, municipalities, school districts and other political subdivisions, and above $150,000 for the State of Colorado. Always confirm the requirement in the bid documents.
