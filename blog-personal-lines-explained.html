Home, Auto & Umbrella Insurance Explained

Three coverages, plain English. Here's what each one actually protects, and why they work best as a set instead of three separate policies from three separate companies.

Ryan Reardon
Insurance Agent · Colorado Springs
September 2026 · 6 min read
🏠

Home Insurance

Home insurance covers more than just the structure of your house. A real policy has four main parts: dwelling coverage (the structure itself), personal property coverage (your belongings), liability protection (if someone is injured on your property or you accidentally damage someone else's property), and loss of use (living expenses if your home becomes temporarily unlivable after a covered loss).

The most common mistake is a dwelling coverage amount that hasn't been updated in years. Construction and repair costs in Colorado Springs have risen significantly, and a policy sized for what your home was worth when you bought it can leave you seriously underinsured if you ever need to rebuild. It's worth reviewing this number regularly, not just accepting the renewal.

Auto Insurance

Colorado requires minimum liability coverage, but "minimum" and "adequate" are rarely the same thing. A real auto policy includes liability (bodily injury and property damage you cause to others), collision (damage to your own vehicle in an accident), comprehensive (theft, weather, animal strikes, and other non-collision damage), and uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage.

That last one matters more in Colorado than most states realize. Colorado has one of the higher rates of uninsured and underinsured drivers in the country — if one hits you, your own UM/UIM coverage is what pays for your injuries and damage. It's inexpensive relative to the protection it provides, and it's one of the first things worth checking on an existing policy.

Umbrella Policy

An umbrella policy is additional liability coverage that sits on top of your home and auto policies. If a claim or lawsuit exceeds the liability limits on either of those policies, the umbrella picks up where they leave off — typically adding $1M or more in additional protection.

It's one of the best values in insurance: often $150–$300 per year for a meaningful amount of additional protection, and it covers situations your other policies may not — certain lawsuits, liability from things like a backyard trampoline or a dog, or damages that simply exceed your standard limits. Anyone with meaningful assets to protect, kids who drive, or just wants real peace of mind should have one.

Why These Three Work Best Together

Home, auto, and umbrella insurance are designed to work as a system. Your umbrella policy's protection depends on your underlying home and auto limits being set correctly. Bundling all three with one carrier typically saves 10–25% versus separate policies, and having one agent who can see your whole picture means gaps between policies get caught before they matter — not after a claim.
